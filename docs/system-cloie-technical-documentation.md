# **INTRODUCTION** {#introduction}

## **1.1 Background of the Study** {#1.1-background-of-the-study}

	Computerization has become essential in education as digital systems enable institutions to collect, organize, and analyze assessment evidence for academic improvement. UNESCO describes learning assessment as part of monitoring and evaluation processes that use multiple information sources to enhance learning outcomes and curriculum quality. Similarly, the International Association of Universities reported 576 responses from 424 institutions across 111 countries in its global COVID-19 survey, highlighting the rapid integration of digital technologies in academic operations (Marinoni et al., 2020; UNESCO, 2026). 

The need for such  system is reinforced by the global shift toward outcomes-based and accreditation-driven education. UNESCO’s global inventory identified 93 case studies from 89 countries monitoring qualifications frameworks, demonstrating the growing role of learning outcomes in shaping education systems. Similarly, ABET reported accrediting 4,773 programs across 930 institutions in 42 countries as of October 1, 2024 (ABET, 2024; UNESCO, 2026). The feasibility of centralized digital assessment platforms is also evident in systems such as Nuventive, used by Penn State to manage learning outcomes, curriculum mapping, and assessment reporting (Penn State Office of Planning, Assessment, and Institutional Research, 2024). These developments show that digital platforms supporting outcomes assessment and continuous improvement are viable and adaptable to the needs of ACD.

Despite these developments, structured outcomes monitoring remains difficult when evaluation is manual or fragmented. At Assumption College of Davao, there is currently no centralized system for managing and tracking learning outcomes across programs. Program heads manually correlate CILO survey results to Program/Graduate Outcomes using spreadsheets or printed forms, a process that can take several hours per evaluation cycle — time that could otherwise be spent on academic planning and curriculum review. Faculty members encode outcomes and evaluation data separately, with no unified view of how course-level results connect to program-level goals. During accreditation preparation, consolidating these scattered records into a coherent evidence report requires significant manual effort from multiple personnel. This fragmented approach increases the risk of data inconsistencies, delays report generation, and makes it difficult to sustain continuous quality improvement across cycles. This gap becomes more significant within Region XI, where CHED RO XI’s official listing shows 106 higher education institutions, reflecting a regional environment in which consistent evidence generation and academic quality monitoring are increasingly important (Commission on Higher Education Regional Office XI, 2024).

Beyond accreditation requirements, curricula must also remain responsive to evolving industry demands. The World Economic Forum reports that 44% of workers’ skills are expected to change within five years, with artificial intelligence and big data among the fastest-growing skill areas (World Economic Forum, 2023). ACD’s evaluation tools already assess whether programs align with professional standards and prepare graduates for employment. These considerations highlight the need for a system that not only documents outcomes for compliance but also helps identify curriculum gaps before programs become misaligned with labor-market demands.

In response, System CLOIE is proposed as a centralized evaluation platform that will manage Program / Graduate Outcomes (GO) and Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILO), gather stakeholder feedback from students, graduates, or alumni, and industry partners, and produce consolidated reports as evidence for program review, quality assurance, and continuous improvement. It is also intended to document outcome gaps, retain historical data for longitudinal analysis, and support action planning, while leaving instructional delivery and individual grading to existing LMS or SIS platforms. By combining computerization, outcomes monitoring, stakeholder feedback, and evidence-based reporting, the study addresses ACD’s need for a more systematic and responsive mechanism for improving curricular quality and alignment with changing industry standards.

## **1.2 Objectives of the Study** {#1.2-objectives-of-the-study}

The study aims to develop the System CLOIE (System for Comprehensive Learning Outcomes and Instructional Evaluation) to support the systematic monitoring and evaluation of Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) and Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) through stakeholder-based feedback, structured data management, and automated reporting for quality assurance and continuous program improvement.	

Specifically, the study aims to:

* To design a system that enables the encoding, organization, and management of and Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) and  Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) aligned with academic programs.  
* Develop a digital platform that facilitates stakeholder-based evaluation through the integration of the required evaluation tools for students, graduating students, alumni, and industry partners.  
* To implement automated reporting features that generate consolidated results on outcome attainment to support program evaluation and accreditation requirements.  
* To ensure secure, confidential, and anonymized data collection while supporting the Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) cycle for continuous quality improvement.

## **1.3 Significance of the Study** {#1.3-significance-of-the-study}

	The development of the System CLOIE will provide a structured digital platform for monitoring learning outcomes and consolidating stakeholder-based evaluation data in higher education. By integrating multiple evaluation tools and generating organized reports, the system can support data-driven decision-making, quality assurance, and continuous program improvement. Specifically, this study will benefit the following:

**Assumption College of Davao.** The system will help the institution establish a more organized and centralized approach to monitoring Program/Graduate Outcomes and Course Intended Learning Outcomes. By providing a single platform for evidence collection and reporting, System CLOIE reduces the administrative burden of preparing accreditation documentation and enables the institution to demonstrate consistent, data-driven quality assurance practices. 

**College Dean.** The System CLOIE dashboard will reduce the Dean's review time for college-wide program performance by providing a consolidated analytics view across all programs — a process that previously required collating separate evaluation reports from each program head. With System CLOIE, the Dean can monitor outcome attainment, identify cross-program gaps, and access accreditation-ready summaries from a single interface. 

**Program Heads.** Instead of manually gathering and tabulating evaluation results from multiple sources, program heads can use System CLOIE to generate outcome attainment reports per evaluation cycle. This allows them to identify specific outcome gaps, compare stakeholder feedback over time, and present structured evidence during program reviews and accreditation visits — significantly reducing preparation time and improving report accuracy. 

**Faculty Members.** Faculty members can use System CLOIE to encode Course Intended Learning Outcomes and view summarized post-term evaluation results for their courses. This gives them direct, structured feedback on how students perceive outcome attainment, supporting reflection on course delivery and alignment with program goals. 

**Students and Graduating Students.** Students benefit from a more responsive academic program that is regularly evaluated against their feedback. Through systematic outcome monitoring, the institution can identify and address gaps in course delivery and program alignment before students complete their degrees, improving the overall quality of their academic experience. 

**Alumni.** Alumni can contribute feedback on how well their academic preparation translated into professional practice through the Alumni Evaluation Tool. Their insights help the institution assess whether program outcomes remain relevant beyond graduation and support evidence-based curriculum updates. 

**Industry Partners.** Industry partners can formally evaluate interns and graduates through the Industry Internship Evaluation Tool, providing structured feedback on workplace readiness, competency alignment, and professional skills. This helps ACD identify whether its program outcomes are aligned with current industry expectations. 

**Future Researchers.** This study may serve as a reference for researchers developing outcome-based evaluation platforms, particularly those designed for college-level quality assurance in Philippine higher education contexts. 

## **1.4 Scope and Delimitation** {#1.4-scope-and-delimitation}

	This section describes the coverage of the proposed System CLOIE and clarifies the boundaries of the study. The scope defines the system’s functions and capabilities, while the delimitation specifies aspects that are intentionally excluded from the system’s design and implementation.

### **1.4.1 Scope** {#1.4.1-scope}

	The study covers the design and development of System CLOIE as a college-level digital evaluation platform for Assumption College of Davao. The system's scope is defined by four operational boundaries: who it serves, what data it collects, how it processes that data, and what outputs it produces. 

In terms of users, System CLOIE supports faculty members, students, graduating students, alumni, industry partners, program heads, the college dean, and system administrators — each with role-specific access and functions. In terms of data, the system collects quantitative ratings and qualitative feedback through four institutional evaluation instruments: the CILO Evaluation Tool, the Graduating Student Exit Survey, the Alumni Evaluation Tool, and the Industry Internship Evaluation Tool. In terms of processing, CLOIE manages outcome structures, stores anonymized responses, computes attainment levels, and generates consolidated reports. In terms of outputs, the system produces outcome attainment reports, stakeholder evaluation summaries, and analytics views accessible to program heads and the college dean for quality assurance, accreditation preparation, and continuous improvement planning. 

### **1.4.2 Delimitation**	 {#1.4.2-delimitation}

	While System CLOIE supports outcome evaluation and reporting, certain functions are intentionally excluded from the system's design to maintain a clearly defined and manageable scope. 

System CLOIE does not manage individual student grades, academic transcripts, or enrollment records, as these remain within the institution's existing Student Information System. It does not deliver instructional content, assignments, or course materials, which are the responsibility of existing Learning Management Systems. The system does not track the academic performance of individual students; its focus is on program-level outcome attainment based on aggregated stakeholder feedback.

More specifically, System CLOIE will not perform predictive analytics on student performance, nor will it automatically generate improvement action plans or curriculum revision recommendations. The system provides the data and structured reports that inform these decisions, but the responsibility for interpreting findings and acting on them remains with program heads, department chairs, and academic administrators. The system also does not replace official institutional quality assurance systems or external accreditation processes, though its reports may serve as supporting evidence for these activities.

## **1.5 Time and Place of the Study** {#1.5-time-and-place-of-the-study}

The study will be conducted at Assumption College of Davao, the client institution where the proposed System CLOIE will be designed and developed. The system is intended to support the monitoring and evaluation of Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) and Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) across the academic programs of the college. Coordination with relevant academic personnel, including program heads and faculty members, will be undertaken to ensure that the design and functionality of the system align with the institution’s requirements for outcome-based education monitoring, stakeholder-based evaluation, and accreditation-related documentation.

The development of the proposed system will occur during the Capstone Project period, from March 2026 to September 2026\. During this period, the proponents will carry out the phases of system analysis, design, development, testing, and documentation. These activities will be conducted in coordination with the academic units of Assumption College of Davao to ensure that the developed system meets the requirements of the institution and supports its quality assurance and continuous improvement initiatives.

## **1.6 Operational Definition of Terms** {#1.6-operational-definition-of-terms}

This section defines key technical terms used in the System CLOIE capstone project to ensure clarity and understanding.

* **Accreditation** – A formal evaluation process conducted by external accrediting bodies to determine whether an academic program or institution meets established standards of educational quality and continuous improvement. In this study, accreditation refers to quality assurance processes that require documented evidence of outcome attainment and program evaluation.  
* **Alumni Evaluation** – A stakeholder-based feedback mechanism in which graduates provide insights regarding the relevance of their academic preparation to their professional experiences. In this study, alumni evaluation is collected through the digital Alumni Evaluation Tool integrated into the System CLOIE.  
* **CILO Evaluation Tool** – A digital instrument used to gather student feedback regarding the attainment of Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) and the effectiveness of instructional delivery. Within this study, the tool is integrated into System CLOIE to support course-level outcome assessment.  
* **Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI)** – An ongoing process of evaluating program performance and implementing improvements based on collected data and feedback. In this study, CQI is supported by System CLOIE through outcome monitoring and evaluation reporting.  
* **Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs)** – Refer to the specific, measurable statements of the knowledge, skills, and values that students are expected to demonstrate upon the successful completion of a particular unit of study, encompassing both General Education and Professional courses. Within System CLOIE, CILOs serve as the micro-level metrics of instructional attainment; they are operationally defined by faculty members and evaluated through student-driven feedback tools to assess immediate academic performance and facilitate the structured mapping of results toward the broader Program / Graduate Outcomes.  
* **Graduating Student Exit Survey** – An evaluation instrument used to collect feedback from students nearing graduation regarding their academic experiences, learning outcomes, and program effectiveness. In this study, the survey is implemented digitally within System CLOIE.  
* **Industry Internship Evaluation** – An evaluation process in which industry partners assess the professional competencies, workplace skills, and readiness of students during internship programs. In this study, the evaluation is conducted using the Industry Internship Evaluation Tool integrated into the System CLOIE.  
* **Outcome Attainment** – The level to which Program / Graduate Outcomes are achieved based on evaluation results collected from various stakeholders. In this study, outcome attainment is measured through aggregated responses and analyzed using reports generated by the System CLOIE.  
* **Outcome-Based Education (OBE)** – An educational approach that focuses on clearly defined learning outcomes and evaluates whether students have achieved the intended knowledge, skills, and competencies upon completion of a course or program. In this study, OBE serves as the guiding framework for monitoring and evaluating learning outcomes through System CLOIE.  
* **Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) Cycle** – A continuous improvement framework used in quality management processes. In this study, the PDCA cycle guides how evaluation results collected by System CLOIE that may support program review and improvement.  
* **Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) –** Refer to the comprehensive and integrated set of knowledge, skills, competencies, and professional attributes that students are expected to demonstrate upon the successful completion of an academic program. In this study, these outcomes serve as the macro-level indicators of program effectiveness and institutional quality; they are operationally monitored, quantified, and analyzed through System CLOIE by aggregating course-level attainment data and synthesizing evaluative feedback from diverse stakeholders (students, alumni, and industry partners) to support continuous quality improvement.  
* **Stakeholder-Based Evaluation** – An evaluation approach that gathers feedback from different groups involved in or affected by an academic program, including students, alumni, and industry partners. In this study, stakeholder-based evaluation is conducted through the digital evaluation instruments integrated into System CLOIE  
* **System User** – An individual authorized to access and interact with System CLOIE. In this study, system users include faculty members, program administrators, students, alumni, and industry partners who participate in encoding data, submitting evaluations, or generating reports.  
* **System CLOIE (System for Comprehensive Learning Outcomes and Instructional Evaluation)** – The proposed digital system developed in this study to support the systematic monitoring and evaluation of Program / Graduate Outcomes and Course Intended Learning Outcomes through stakeholder-based feedback, structured data management, and automated reporting.

## ***Figure 1\.** Conceptual Framework* {#figure-1.-conceptual-framework}

The conceptual framework of System CLOIE is anchored on the Input–Process–Output (IPO) model, integrated with a Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) feedback loop based on the Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) cycle. This structure ensures that academic evaluation is not merely a static collection of data but a dynamic system for iterative institutional development.

**Input** The Input phase comprises data entries and system configurations originating from four hierarchical user levels. The System Administrator and College Dean establish the system’s foundation through full CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) access to the core database catalog, specifically for programs, courses, and evaluation tool templates. Program Heads provide program-level parameters by encoding the specific Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) required for their respective departments. Faculty Members fulfill a dual-input role, responsible for encoding Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) for their assigned subjects and executing bulk uploads to populate student respondents. Finally, the core evaluation data is provided by Stakeholders—including Students, Alumni, and Industry Partners—who interact with the system by completing digital evaluation tools containing both quantitative and qualitative prompts.

**Process** The Process phase governs the transformation of inputs within the centralized CLOIE cloud environment through a strict, top-to-bottom sequential flow. The workflow begins at the **Catalog Hub**, which synchronizes all academic and system data in real-time. This data is then managed by the **Outcome Mapping Engine**, which establishes the logical relational mapping between course-bound CILOs and broader GOs. Before deployment, the **Evaluation Template Engine** allows academic leaders to configure and customize specific tool instances. Once configured, the **Participant Processing Engine** filters uploaded lists to generate targeted deployment rosters, which the **Deployment Router** then pushes directly to stakeholder dashboards. Upon submission, responses pass into **Anonymized Storage**, where identifiable data is stripped for privacy and records are securely locked. The processed data is then finalized through **Dual Analytics Engines**: the Qualitative NLP Processor tokenizes textual feedback to identify prominent themes for word cloud generation, while the *Quantitative Processor* computes mean scores and aggregates attainment levels while flagging any data inconsistencies.

**Output** The Output phase synthesizes processed data into actionable evidence of academic performance. The system generates **Role-Scoped Single-Program Dashboards** to provide Program Heads and Faculty with focused monitoring capabilities. Simultaneously, a **Multi-Department Aggregate View** is provided to the College Dean for college-wide oversight. Beyond these live interfaces, the system produces standardized, exportable **Generated Documents**, specifically *Outcome Attainment Reports* and *Stakeholder Summaries*, which serve as the primary evidence for academic auditing and accreditation.

**Feedback Loop** The framework is completed by a feedback loop driven by the principles of Continuous Quality Improvement. The insights and reports generated in the Output phase serve as the "Check" and "Act" components of the **PDCA cycle**. This academic evidence informs institutional reviews and curricular adjustments, which are subsequently reintegrated into the system as revised inputs for the next evaluation cycle, ensuring a sustained loop of evidence-based improvement.

## **2.1 Related Literature** {#2.1-related-literature}

## **2.1.1 Outcome-Based Education in Higher Education** {#2.1.1-outcome-based-education-in-higher-education}

Outcome-Based Education (OBE) emphasizes the measurable attainment of knowledge, skills, and competencies rather than mere content coverage. In such frameworks, programs must clearly define expected graduate capabilities, identify evidence of attainment, and use findings for review and improvement. This is consistent with current accreditation guidance, which frames assessment in terms of identifying, collecting, and preparing evidence for evaluating student outcomes and continuous improvement (ABET, 2024). In the Philippine context, the University of the Philippines (UP) quality assurance system similarly emphasizes evidence-based program review and recurring evaluation (University of the Philippines, 2025). For CLOIE, OBE is the core foundation because the system is intended to monitor GOs, and CILOs through organized outcomes definitions, measures, mappings, and reports. This supports CLOIE's design as a platform that makes OBE evidence visible, manageable, and consistently available for program review. 

### **2.1.2 Learning Outcomes Assessment** {#2.1.2-learning-outcomes-assessment}

Learning outcomes assessment is the process through which institutions determine whether intended competencies have been attained. It goes beyond grades and includes direct and indirect measures, rubrics, curriculum maps, and attainment thresholds. Goss (2022) notes that student learning outcomes assessment is essential but complex because it requires institutions to translate broad goals into assessable evidence and use that evidence for decision-making. This is directly relevant to CLOIE because the proposed system is designed to support the outcomes-assessment workflow by collecting stakeholder responses, mapping evidence to outcomes, aggregating data, and producing organized reports for academic leaders. 

### **2.1.3 Quality Assurance in Higher Education** {#2.1.3-quality-assurance-in-higher-education}

Quality assurance refers to the policies, procedures, and evidence-based practices used to maintain and enhance educational quality. It typically involves internal and external review, standards compliance, documentation, and follow-up actions. Javed and Alenezi (2023) argue that sustainable quality assurance depends on strong data management, indicators, and systematic documentation rather than fragmented or compliance-only practices. UP’s QA framework reflects this by combining internal and external QA, self-assessment, evidence portals, and review schedules (University of the Philippines, 2025). For CLOIE, this literature is important because it positions learning outcomes monitoring within the broader institutional use of evidence for accreditation, program review, and quality assurance.

## 

### **2.1.4 Continuous Quality Improvement in Academic Programs** {#2.1.4-continuous-quality-improvement-in-academic-programs}

Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) extends quality assurance by focusing on recurring cycles of review, action, and reassessment. The PDCA framework is widely used because it connects intended outcomes, implementation, evidence review, and corrective or enhancement actions. Samuel and Farrer (2025) explain that PDCA helps universities move from compliance-oriented reporting toward iterative improvement when evidence is systematically collected and interpreted. CLOIE is relevant in this context because it is designed to strengthen the “check” phase by collecting stakeholder evaluations, aggregating outcome results, and generating reports that program heads and deans can use before deciding on improvements. This suggests CLOIE should support multiple stakeholder groups to capture the complete picture of program effectiveness — a design principle reflected in its four integrated evaluation instruments. 

### **2.1.5 Curriculum Relevance and Industry Alignment** {#2.1.5-curriculum-relevance-and-industry-alignment}

Curriculum relevance is a major concern in higher education because labor-market needs and skill expectations change rapidly. The World Economic Forum (2023) reports that 44 percent of workers’ skills are expected to be disrupted within five years, with analytical thinking, creative thinking, and technology literacy among the fastest-growing areas. Mahalingam (2024) further shows that industry-driven curriculum review can reveal competency gaps when subject matter experts participate in syllabus and outcomes evaluation. For CLOIE, this is highly relevant because feedback from students, graduates, and industry partners can help determine whether outcomes remain aligned with current professional and industry expectations.

### **2.1.6 Graduate Employability as an Indicator of Program Relevance** {#2.1.6-graduate-employability-as-an-indicator-of-program-relevance}

Graduate employability is often used as a practical indicator of program relevance because it reflects whether graduates can apply their learning in work, licensure, further studies, or related contexts. Tracer studies commonly examine employment status, job-field alignment, workplace readiness, and the usefulness of curricular experiences. As a quality assurance instrument, employability evidence helps institutions determine whether outcomes remain meaningful beyond classroom completion. This matters to CLOIE because the system is intended to include graduate and industry perspectives so that outcomes monitoring can be linked to readiness, adaptability, and relevance in real work environments.

These studies are directly relevant to CLOIE because they show that outcomes monitoring should also consider graduate readiness, employment experience, and the real-world relevance of academic preparation.

Studies on stakeholder-based evaluation indicate that no single respondent group can provide a complete picture of academic quality. (2025) demonstrate that graduates contribute evidence on preparedness, relevance, and job alignment. Student evaluations, meanwhile, remain important because they provide course-level and program-experience perspectives while learning is still ongoing. These findings imply that CLOIE becomes more credible by integrating feedback from students, graduates or alumni, and industry partners.

## **2.4 Synthesis of Reviewed Literature, Studies, and Systems** {#2.4-synthesis-of-reviewed-literature,-studies,-and-systems}

The reviewed literature establishes that the need for CLOIE is rooted in well-recognized higher education priorities. OBE requires clearly defined outcomes and evidence of attainment; quality assurance requires organized documentation and review; continuous quality improvement depends on recurring feedback cycles; curriculum relevance requires responsiveness to industry and labor-market change; and graduate employability serves as an external indicator of whether program outcomes remain meaningful beyond graduation. Taken together, this literature shows that modern higher education increasingly expects decisions to be supported by structured evidence rather than by anecdotal impressions or fragmented records.

The reviewed studies confirm that these expectations are difficult to satisfy manually. Research on outcomes assessment shows that scattered tools and conventional processes often fail to support accreditation-oriented measurement or sustained interpretation of attainment data. Studies on curriculum alignment, tracer research, and stakeholder evaluation show that multiple respondent groups are necessary to understand program relevance and graduate readiness. Learning analytics studies further show that dashboards and analytic views improve the usefulness of educational data when they help users interpret patterns, gaps, and priorities.

The reviewed systems demonstrate that digital platforms can operationalize these functions in practice. Penn State and Oklahoma State show the feasibility of integrating outcomes documentation, dashboards, reporting, and action planning. UP shows that a Philippine university can centralize QA processes and multiple feedback instruments. CHED’s regional systems show that monitoring and analytics are also valued at the oversight level. Tracer-related systems show that graduate data can be collected and interpreted more systematically through dedicated portals and analytics-supported systems.

Despite these strengths, what remains insufficiently addressed is the need for a focused, department- or college-level platform that combines learning outcomes evaluation, GO/CILO monitoring, stakeholder-based evaluation, qualitative feedback, basic statistical analysis, and report generation for program heads and deans in one environment. The reviewed platforms are often institution-wide, tracer-specific, or regulator-facing; they do not consistently integrate all of the functions required by the current study in a scoped, scalable platform for ongoing academic program monitoring.

For this reason, the development of CLOIE is justified. The proposed system is intended to provide a more systematic, transparent, and auditable mechanism for monitoring outcomes attainment, consolidating stakeholder evidence, producing analytics and reports, and supporting quality assurance, accreditation, and curriculum review. Its value lies not in replacing academic judgment, but in making evidence available in a structured way so that program heads and deans can interpret outcome gaps, examine program relevance, and act on documented findings.

## **3.1 Software Development Framework** {#3.1-software-development-framework}

The development of System CLOIE follows a Kanban-based Agile software development framework. This framework was adopted to provide the proponents with a structured yet flexible method for planning, organizing, monitoring, and completing the system. Since System CLOIE involves several interconnected modules, including authentication, role-based access, academic structure management, outcomes management, evaluation instrument management, response collection, analytics, reporting, and documentation, the development process required a framework that could clearly visualize work progress and support continuous task management.

Agile development emphasizes iterative progress, frequent monitoring, and adaptability throughout the software development process. Within this Agile context, the proponents selected Kanban as the primary work-management method. Kanban is commonly used to visualize work, limit work in progress, manage workflow, and continuously improve the delivery process. The Official Guide to the Kanban Method describes Kanban as a method for managing knowledge work by making work and its movement through a workflow visible, limiting parallel work, managing flow, using feedback loops, and improving the system continuously (Kanban University, 2022). In software development, this makes Kanban useful for organizing tasks, identifying bottlenecks, and maintaining a steady pace of work.

The proponents selected Kanban because it is suitable for a small capstone development team working within client-established requirements and academic deadlines. Unlike sprint-based approaches that require fixed time-boxed iterations, Kanban allows tasks to move continuously across workflow stages as they become ready, active, reviewed, tested, and completed. This approach is appropriate for System CLOIE because the system requires the simultaneous management of software development tasks, documentation outputs, testing activities, and project-management deliverables. The use of Kanban helped the proponents monitor the overall progress of the project without introducing excessive project-management overhead.

In Project CLOIE, Kanban was implemented through a Notion-based task board. Each task card represented a specific project activity, such as developing a system module, designing a user interface, writing documentation, preparing diagrams, testing a feature, or reviewing completed work. The board was organized into five workflow stages: Backlog, Ready, In Progress, For Review/Testing, and Done. These stages allowed the proponents to track the status of each task and determine which activities should be prioritized, actively worked on, reviewed, or marked as complete.

### **3.2.2 Functional Requirements** {#3.2.2-functional-requirements}

	The functional requirements of CLOIE are organized into two categories based on their role in the PDCA-based quality improvement cycle.

**Core for Accreditation Evidence** — These functions directly support the generation of structured, traceable evidence for program evaluation and accreditation: outcome encoding and management (GOs, CILOs), CILO-to-GO outcome mapping, evaluation instrument management and versioning, response collection and one-response enforcement, and outcome attainment reporting.

**Supporting Functions** — These functions enhance the system's usability, analytical depth, and operational integrity but are not primary accreditation deliverables: qualitative feedback handling and word cloud generation, role-based access controls, program-specific instrument variants, predefined response prompt configuration, and administrative management of academic structures.

This categorization reflects the Kanban-based development priority used in the project. Core functions were treated as must-complete deliverables for the Prototype Defense, while supporting functions are staged as secondary priorities aligned with capacity and timeline.

Functional requirements define the specific services, behaviors, and operations that CLOIE must provide. These requirements describe how the system should behave when users interact with it, how data should be managed, and what outputs should be produced. In the context of this study, the functional requirements are organized according to the major modules and processes expected from CLOIE. The following are the capabilities CLOIE will serve:

* User and Access Management  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to access the system through secure authentication.  
  * The system shall support student sign-up and login using official ACD domain Gmail accounts to ensure that only authorized Assumption College of Davao students can access student-specific evaluation functions.  
  * The system shall support role-based access for faculty members, students, graduating students, alumni, industry partners, program heads, college dean, and system administrators.  
  * The system shall restrict access to functions, forms, and reports based on assigned user roles.  
  * The system shall collect and manage only the necessary academic classification details required for access control, form targeting, and report grouping, such as program, year level, section/class group, and course association where applicable.  
  * The system shall allow users to be assigned to the appropriate program, year level, section/class group, and course, depending on their role and academic relationship to the evaluation process.  
  * The system shall allow faculty members to access only the courses, class groups, evaluation forms, and reports relevant to their assigned teaching responsibilities.  
  * The system shall allow students to access only the evaluation forms assigned to their program, year level, section/class group, and course, where applicable.  
  * The system shall allow graduating students, alumni, and industry partners to access only the evaluation forms intended for their specific stakeholder group and authorized evaluation cycle.  
  * The system shall enforce controlled visibility of evaluation forms based on user role and academic association so that unauthorized users cannot view or answer forms not intended for them.  
  * The system shall allow administrators to create, modify, assign roles to, deactivate, and delete user accounts.  
  * The system shall maintain secure user-session handling and access control throughout the use of the system.

    

* Outcomes Management  
  * Program-Level Outcomes Management  
    * The system shall allow authorized program heads or academic administrators to encode, and organize Program / Graduate Outcomes (GOs) associated with specific academic programs.

    * The system shall associate each program-level outcome with the corresponding academic program.  
  * Course-Level Outcomes Management  
    * The system shall allow faculty members to encode, organize, and manage Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) for the courses they handle.

    * The system shall allow course-level outcomes to be maintained and updated by the authorized faculty member responsible for the course.  
  * Outcome Mapping / Cascade Management  
    * The system shall support the mapping or alignment of CILOs to the appropriate program-level outcomes (GOs) so that course-level attainment can be traced to broader program-level goals.  
    * The system shall allow authorized program heads or academic administrators to review, maintain, or approve these outcome mappings.  
    * The system shall preserve the relationship between course-level and program-level outcomes for use in evaluation, reporting, and longitudinal analysis.  
  * Outcome Record Management  
    * The system shall associate outcomes with the corresponding academic program, course, and evaluation context where applicable.  
    * The system shall retain stored outcomes and their mappings for future evaluation, reporting, and longitudinal analysis.

* Evaluation Instrument Management  
  * The system shall provide the default institutional evaluation instruments of Assumption College of Davao for the appropriate stakeholder groups, including the CILO Evaluation Tool, Graduating Student Exit Survey Tool, Alumni Evaluation Tool, and Industry Partner Internship Evaluation Tool.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to assign the appropriate evaluation instruments to the corresponding programs, courses, stakeholder groups, and evaluation cycles.  
  * The system shall support the digital delivery of evaluation instruments for the intended stakeholder groups without exposing unrelated forms to unauthorized users.

  * The system shall prevent direct modification of an evaluation instrument version that is already active or has recorded responses, unless a new version is created.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to activate a new version of an evaluation instrument for future evaluation cycles without affecting completed or ongoing cycles.  
  * The system shall support institution-approved rating scales appropriate to a given evaluation instrument or stakeholder group, while maintaining an internally consistent scoring structure for analysis and reporting.  
  * The system shall support optional qualitative feedback fields within the integrated evaluation instruments.  
  * The system shall support optional predefined response prompts for selected open-ended questions in applicable evaluation instruments.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to configure predefined response prompts for supported qualitative items.  
  * The system shall allow predefined response prompts to be associated with specific open-ended questions, evaluation instruments, program-specific variants, and stakeholder groups where applicable.

    

* Evaluation Cycle Management  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to create and manage evaluation cycles by academic term, school year, or evaluation period.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to open, schedule, and close evaluation cycles according to the intended evaluation timeline.  
  * The system shall allow evaluation forms to be activated and assigned to a specific course, program, year level, section/class group, stakeholder group, and evaluation cycle, where applicable.  
  * The system shall ensure that only the intended respondents can view and answer the evaluation forms assigned to them.  
  * The system shall prevent students and other respondents from viewing or answering evaluation forms not associated with their assigned course, class group, program, stakeholder role, or evaluation context.  
  * The system shall enforce one response per evaluator per assigned evaluation form and evaluation cycle, when applicable.  
  * The system shall associate each evaluation cycle with the specific evaluation instrument version assigned to it.  
  * The system shall associate each activated evaluation form with the correct target group, academic context, and evaluation cycle to preserve response accuracy and traceability.  
  * The system shall prevent direct modification of an evaluation instrument version that is already active or has recorded responses, unless a new version is created.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to activate a new version of an evaluation instrument for future evaluation cycles without affecting completed or ongoing cycles.  
  * The system shall preserve the historical relationship among the evaluation cycle, assigned instrument version, target respondents, and submitted responses for future reporting and longitudinal analysis.


* Response Collection and Processing  
  * The system shall accept quantitative and qualitative responses from authorized stakeholders through the appropriate evaluation forms.  
  * The system shall allow only eligible respondents assigned to a specific evaluation form, stakeholder group, course, program, year level, section/class group, or evaluation cycle to answer that form.  
  * The system shall validate required fields and response completeness before allowing the respondent to proceed to final review.  
  * The system shall support a preview or review modal/page that displays the respondent’s completed answers before final submission.  
  * The system shall require a confirmation prompt before permanently recording a response.  
  * The system shall allow final submission only after the respondent has reviewed and confirmed the completed response.  
  * The system shall securely store submitted responses in the system database as finalized records once submission has been confirmed.  
  * The system shall preserve the submitted response as a finalized record after confirmation and prevent unintended modification of the recorded response.  
  * The system shall support response integrity while protecting respondent identity through appropriate confidentiality and access-control mechanisms.  
  * The system shall process evaluation responses and organize them according to program outcomes, graduate outcomes, course outcomes, stakeholder groups, and academic periods.  
  * The system shall associate each submitted response with the exact evaluation instrument version used during the corresponding evaluation cycle.  
  * The system shall preserve historical response records even when newer customized versions of the same evaluation instrument are later created.  
  * The system shall allow respondents to answer supported open-ended questions by manually typing their response without selecting any predefined prompt.  
  * The system shall allow respondents to click one or more predefined response prompts for selected open-ended questions.  
  * The system shall automatically populate the open-ended textbox with the selected predefined prompt.  
  * The system shall concatenate multiple selected predefined prompts in the textbox, separated by commas.  
  * The system shall allow respondents to manually edit, remove, or extend textbox content after predefined prompts are inserted.  
  * The system shall store the final qualitative response entered by the respondent, whether it is manually typed, prompt-assisted, or a combination of both.  
  * The system shall preserve the final user-submitted qualitative text regardless of how the response was composed.

  * The system shall present reports that reflect the relationship between program-level outcomes and the attainment of related CILOs.  
  * The system shall support analytics that show whether program-level outcomes are supported by the attainment of mapped course-level outcomes.  
  * The system shall flag or highlight inconsistencies or possible contradictions between program-level outcome results and related course-level outcome results, such as cases where broader outcomes appear attained while the supporting CILOs are not sufficiently attained.  
  * The system shall present stakeholder-based comparative results for program review, quality assurance, and outcome monitoring.  
  * The system shall retain historical records to support trend analysis and longitudinal review across evaluation cycles.  
  * The system shall produce reports that may be used as documented evidence for accreditation, program evaluation, and Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI).  
  * The system shall identify outcome gaps, recurring weaknesses, and areas requiring review by academic leadership.  
  * The system shall support CQI-oriented reporting by presenting findings that may guide curriculum review, program improvement, and action planning.  
  * The system shall allow authorized users to access reports appropriate to their role and responsibility.  
  * The system shall support qualitative feedback analytics through word cloud generation based on collected open-ended responses.  
  * The system shall identify and visualize frequently occurring words or terms from qualitative responses.

  * The system shall preserve the original submitted qualitative text even when the responses are processed for visualization, summarization, or reporting.

  * Respondent feedback data shall be stored, processed, and reported only for the evaluation and quality assurance purposes for which it was collected, and shall not be shared with unauthorized parties or used for purposes beyond institutional program review and accreditation support.  
  * The system shall support informed participation by ensuring that respondents are made aware of the evaluation's purpose before submitting responses.

* Confidentiality and Privacy  
  * The system shall support confidential and anonymized collection of stakeholder responses where appropriate.  
  * The system shall ensure that evaluation responses are reported only in aggregate form when confidentiality is required.  
  * The system shall protect respondent information from unauthorized disclosure.  
  * The system shall support controlled evaluation periods and submission restrictions to preserve data integrity.  
  * The system shall support anonymized reporting for sensitive responses where necessary.  
  * The system shall ensure that qualitative and quantitative outputs presented for review follow the confidentiality rules defined by the system.  
  * The system shall limit the exposure of sensitive respondent information in reports, dashboards, and qualitative analysis views.

* Reliability  
  * The system shall preserve the integrity of evaluation instruments and their associated responses across multiple versions and evaluation cycles.  
  * The system shall ensure that previously used instrument versions remain unchanged once they have recorded responses, in order to maintain reporting consistency and data traceability.  
  * The system shall reliably preserve the final qualitative response as edited and submitted by the respondent.  
  * The system shall ensure that prompt-assisted text population does not unexpectedly corrupt, overwrite, or distort manually entered responses.  
  * The system shall preserve finalized responses after confirmation so that submitted records remain stable and reliable.  
  * The system shall preserve traceable mappings between Course Intended Learning Outcomes (CILOs) and program-level outcomes for reporting and analysis.  
  * The system shall maintain consistency of reports across outcome mappings, instrument versions, and evaluation cycles.  
      
* Scalability  
  * The system shall support multiple academic programs, courses, outcomes, stakeholder groups, and program-specific evaluation instrument variants without requiring major redesign.  
  * The system shall be capable of handling increasing response volume and instrument complexity across evaluation cycles.  
  * The database structure shall support expansion across the academic programs of Assumption College of Davao, including program-level customization of default evaluation instruments.  
  * The system shall also support scalable management of optional qualitative prompt configurations across supported instruments and programs.

* Availability

  * The system shall remain usable for students, faculty, alumni, and industry partners when accessed from different locations.

  * The system shall be suitable for implementation as a web-based Progressive Web Application (PWA) to improve accessibility and app-like usability across devices.

* Maintainability  
  * The system shall be designed in a way that allows future refinement, updates, and expansion of features.

  * The system shall support maintainable management of default evaluation instruments, program-specific variants, and instrument version history.

  * The system shall support maintainable configuration of predefined qualitative response prompts for supported open-ended questions.  
  * The system shall allow future updating or refinement of qualitative prompt options and qualitative analytics features without requiring major redesign of the system.  
  * The system shall support long-term curriculum and form revision cycles without breaking historical records or report traceability.  
  * The system shall support maintainable form versioning and outcome mapping as programs evolve over time.  
  * The system shall allow approved evaluation instruments to be adapted flexibly without disrupting previously recorded responses and historical data.

These non-functional requirements support the use of CLOIE as a college-level digital evaluation platform. They also align with the chosen cross-platform direction of the project, where a responsive web/PWA architecture is preferred because CLOIE is multi-user, form-heavy, report-oriented, centralized, and not hardware-dependent. A web/PWA approach also supports ease of access, simpler maintenance, and efficient development within capstone constraints.

This makes adoption more practical because it improves familiar workflows.

Operational feasibility is also supported by the system’s defined scope and accessibility. CLOIE is limited to evaluation, monitoring, and reporting, which allows it to complement existing systems rather than replace LMS, SIS, or grading platforms. ### **3.3.4 Schedule Feasibility** {#3.3.4-schedule-feasibility}

CLOIE is schedule-feasible within the academic calendar, covering the period from March to September 2026, anchored by defined academic milestones: the Title Defense on March 9–10, the Outline Defense on April 27–29, the Prototype Demonstration tentatively in the second week of July, and the Final Defense in the second week of September 2026\.

During the Initiation and Planning Phase (March 2026), the team will conduct client meetings, gather requirements, perform feasibility analysis, and finalize the technology stack and risk assessment. In the System Analysis and Design Phase (March to May 2026), user requirements and stories will be specified, database and system architecture will be designed, and interface prototypes will be developed using Figma, establishing a solid foundation prior to the Outline Defense.

The Core System Development Phase (May to July 2026), the largest phase at approximately 31 percent of the total work, covers environment setup and authentication, the outcomes management module (GO/CILO), the evaluation instrument builder, and the stakeholder response collection interface, all managed through a Kanban-based workflow leading up to the Prototype Demonstration. This is followed by the Analytics, Reporting, and Testing Phase (July to September 2026), which focuses on the quantitative and qualitative analytics engines, word cloud generation, program head dashboard, and end-to-end and user acceptance testing.

The project concludes with the Final Documentation and Defense Phase (August to September 2026), covering manuscript completion, appendix compilation, and defense rehearsal. Because Kanban supports continuous workflow and prioritized task completion without rigid sprint structures, the proponents consider CLOIE achievable within the given 210-day academic timeframe.

## **3.7 Implementation Plan** {#3.7-implementation-plan}

	The Implementation Plan outlines the structured process of deploying the System CLOIE at Assumption College of Davao, covering the transition from final development to full operational use. It begins with the proponents finalizing the production deployment to the cloud hosting environment, including domain configuration, Supabase database setup, and verification of security settings in coordination with the institution's IT personnel. Once the environment is ready, user accounts are created and configured for faculty members, program heads, the college dean, students, alumni, and industry partners based on official ACD records. Following account setup, key users undergo an orientation and training session covering system navigation, outcomes encoding, evaluation cycle management, and report generation, supported by a simple user guide.

After training, the initial evaluation cycle is launched under proponent supervision, with forms assigned to stakeholder groups and respondents guided through access and completion. Following the initial cycle, the proponents conduct a post-deployment review to address any issues and formally hand over all documentation, source code, deployment credentials, and configuration guides to designated ACD personnel. This cycle ensures that the system remains functional, secure, and ready for sustained institutional use.
